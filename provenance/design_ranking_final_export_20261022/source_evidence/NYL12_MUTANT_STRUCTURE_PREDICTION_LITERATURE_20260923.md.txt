# Mutant Structure Prediction Literature Note

Date: 2026-09-23

## Bottom line

The literature does not support a simple conclusion that our static-model failures are caused mainly by Boltz-2 being unable to model mutants. Mutant modeling is a real limitation for AF2/AF3/Boltz-like predictors, especially when the functional effect depends on subtle side-chain packing, loop shifts, altered conformational ensembles, or ligand-coupled active-site preorganization. However, recent work also shows that predicted WT and mutant structures can be useful when used as hypotheses or as inputs to downstream variant-effect pipelines.

For the Nyl12 commercial-polyamide benchmark, the more defensible interpretation is that mutant-modeling uncertainty is one contributor within a harder composite problem: flexible repetitive polyamide ligands, uncertain substrate register and orientation, finite charged oligomer artifacts, catalytically restrictive near-attack geometry, local minimization rather than broad ensemble sampling, approximate Rosetta `interface_delta` scoring, and indirect lysate product measurements.

## Citation-network check around the McBride/Tlusty effective-strain work

The key missed item was the PRL follow-up by McBride and Tlusty in 2024. The 2023 PRL paper introduced effective strain as a local deformation metric and reported that AF2-predicted WT-vs-mutant deformation correlates with experimental structural and phenotypic effects across many proteins. The 2024 PRL paper extends that idea to stability, arguing that AF-predicted deformation contains information about energy-landscape perturbations and that averaging multiple AF structures helps separate mutation-induced deformation from stochastic prediction noise.

The cited-reference neighborhood reinforces two points already relevant to our manuscript. First, pLDDT or global RMSD are weak diagnostics for mutation effects; local deformation or strain-style metrics can be more informative. Second, several cited and citing papers emphasize that AF2/ColabFold can sometimes sample alternate conformations or encode dynamical information, but this is context-dependent and not equivalent to predicting catalytic activity or ligand-bound productive geometry. I did not find a newer citing paper that overturns our interpretation. The most relevant addition is the 2024 PRL stability/energy-landscape paper, plus a clearer statement that our workflow did not use local strain metrics and therefore should not be judged as a direct test of that approach.

## Manuscript-ready summary

A related limitation concerns the WT-vs-mutant comparison itself. AF2/AF3/Boltz-family models are very useful for generating structural hypotheses, but the literature is mixed on whether independently predicted WT and single-mutant structures reliably capture subtle functional consequences of mutations. AF2-derived structures can be useful as inputs to downstream variant-effect predictors, and AF3-generated complexes have supported protein-protein mutant binding-energy benchmarks. However, direct comparison of predicted WT and mutant structures is not a validated route to subtle activity prediction: AF2 can be relatively insensitive to single substitutions, confidence-score changes correlate weakly or not at all with many experimental stability and function measurements, and current predictors often miss alternative conformational states. The effective-strain literature is an important counterpoint: local deformation metrics extracted from ensembles of AF2 predictions can capture average single-mutation effects and stability trends better than pLDDT or global RMSD. However, that approach is aimed at mutation-induced protein deformation, not at ranking catalytic enzyme-polyamide complexes by static protein-ligand interaction energy.

Recent Boltz-2 applications to mutant drug-binding panels are encouraging, but they address relatively conventional drug-receptor binding problems rather than enzyme-substrate catalysis with large repetitive polymer fragments. Broader co-folding benchmarks also show that ligand-pose success and confidence metrics do not necessarily imply conformational correctness, physical validity, or energetic resolution. Thus, disagreement between our static Nyl12 models and experiment should not be attributed simply to Boltz-2 being unable to handle mutants. Mutant-specific conformational effects are plausible in some cases, especially for substitutions that perturb active-site loops or substrate-channel shape, but the larger challenge is the compounded task of predicting flexible polyamide register, catalytic near-attack geometry, local relaxation, and lysate activity from static co-folded complexes.

## Implications for Nyl12

- Treat static Boltz-2/OpenMM/Rosetta models as hypotheses, not activity predictors.
- Interpret WT-vs-mutant score differences only within matched substrate/product/register comparisons.
- Use catalytic geometry filters because productive hydrolysis is more restrictive than generic binding.
- Avoid claiming that Boltz-2 cannot handle mutants; the literature supports a more nuanced statement.
- Treat failures as arising from multiple coupled uncertainties: mutant conformational response, substrate register, polyamide chemistry, scoring, and assay biology.
- In future work, compare direct variant co-folding against WT-template mutation-and-relaxation controls, broader local side-chain/loop sampling, and experimental kinetic validation for focused variants.

## 2026-10-15 effective-strain feasibility and software installation

Effective strain can be calculated for the existing Boltz single-mutant models.
It measures local protein deformation between two structures or ensembles,
after optimal local rotation of C-alpha neighborhood vectors. The original
method uses a 13 A neighborhood. It is not merely a change in scalar pair
distances, a physical elastic energy, or an independent model-accuracy score.
Large strain can indicate either a real structural response or prediction
variability; small strain can indicate either a small response or failure to
capture one. Without experimental mutant structures, it cannot establish
which mutant structures are correct. Applying it to Boltz oligomeric complexes
is an exploratory extension of the published work, principally based on AF2.

The authors' software is PDAnalysis, https://github.com/mirabdi/PDAnalysis,
MIT license, version 0.0.3 in the inspected source. The downloaded revision is
`78eb002c8b180c9cf2238f6c1ca932e5e1e64bf2`, under
`external/PDAnalysis`. An isolated uv project under `tools/pdanalysis` contains
the dependency declaration and lock file; this does not change the inference
or relaxation environments. The source is an editable local dependency, so
reproduction requires the same checkout revision as well as the uv lock file.
The first smoke test exposed upstream use of `np.in1d`, removed in recent
NumPy releases. The isolated environment therefore constrains NumPy to
`>=1.26.4,<2`; the upstream scientific implementation remains unmodified.
The upstream console entry point refers to a missing `PDAnalysis.main` module;
use the Python API or the repository's top-level `main.py` instead.

Reproduction from the project root:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/mirabdi/PDAnalysis.git external/PDAnalysis
git -C external/PDAnalysis checkout 78eb002c8b180c9cf2238f6c1ca932e5e1e64bf2
uv sync --project tools/pdanalysis --locked
uv run --project tools/pdanalysis python scripts/check_nyl12_pdanalysis.py
```

The clone command is for a fresh installation. The scripted installation check
uses the original WT PA6 model 40 alpha chain A and tests self-comparison,
invariance under rigid rotation/translation, and sensitivity to a local 1 A
displacement. Its report is
`output_nyl12_effective_strain_setup_20261015/installation_checks.json`.
These checks establish basic software behavior, not biological validation.
All three passed on 2026-10-15: maximum identity strain was 4.15e-15,
maximum strain after rigid motion was 3.80e-15, and a 1 A displacement at
residue 101 produced strain 0.1064 there. Ruff lint and format checks passed.
The locked compatible environment uses NumPy 1.26.4 and SciPy 1.17.1.

### Recommended initial analysis, not yet executed

1. Use all 50 original/unrelaxed models per sequence within each substrate
   representation separately. Compare the protein C-alpha neighborhoods;
   exclude ligand atoms from strain, while recognizing that protein
   conformations were predicted in the ligand's presence. Removing ligand
   coordinates does not convert a complex prediction into an apo prediction.
2. Validate chain correspondence and residue identities before calculation.
   The upstream PDB reader retains residue numbers but does not retain chain
   identity in its coordinate indexing. Repeated numbering across chains can
   silently overwrite coordinates. An adapter must assign a unique global
   index to every protein residue and preserve a reversible chain/residue map.
   Check the expected four copies of the mutation explicitly; chain labels
   alone do not establish structural correspondence in a symmetric assembly.
3. Preserve all eight physical subunits and inter-subunit neighborhoods for
   the primary assembly analysis. Also examine within-subunit strain to
   distinguish backbone deformation from changes in subunit arrangement.
   Report sites and chains separately before a descriptive symmetry average;
   the four copies in one model are not independent replicates.
4. Use the upstream locally averaged-neighborhood implementation for ensemble
   comparisons, rather than globally averaging PDB coordinates. Compare the
   mutant-WT signal with equal-size independent WT-WT ensemble splits and
   mutant-mutant splits. Repeated splits are a noise diagnostic, not new
   independent observations. Assess convergence at 5, 10, 20, and 25 models
   against the full 50-model comparison, with resampling uncertainty.
5. Begin with the paper's confidence cutoff of 70 as an explicit transferred
   convention, not a validated Boltz threshold. Define common residue masks
   across comparisons and report coverage. Original Boltz PDBs preserve
   confidence in B-factor fields; the Amber/OpenMM relaxed PDBs have zero
   B-factors, so applying a confidence cutoff to them would reject everything.
   A relaxed sensitivity analysis must transfer confidence through a validated
   original-to-relaxed residue mapping. Inspect unmasked/high-confidence
   sensitivity separately, especially for active-site loops.
6. Summarize deformation at each mutated residue, its protein neighborhood
   within 15 A, and predeclared active-site regions. These are descriptive
   summaries; the paper's strain-stability relationships do not supply a
   calibration for Nyl12 lysate activity. Avoid selecting regions or strain
   thresholds by whichever combination best fits the eight activity values.
7. Treat post-relaxation models and geometry-passing models as sensitivity
   analyses. Catalytic filtering is not the primary inclusion rule for strain:
   it conditions on substrate geometry, changes the sampled pose distribution,
   and yields unequal and sometimes tiny ensembles (e.g. PA6 V111C, 1/50).
   Report selection and sample counts, and do not compare a single accepted
   mutant to a 50-model WT ensemble as evidence of mutation-induced strain.

### Apo controls and sampling recommendation

Start with the existing ensembles before generating additional predictions.
If interpretation remains dominated by substrate pose, use an apo Boltz
control with the same mature hetero-octamer and matched MSA/settings across WT
and all eight mutants, without substrate-directed contacts. This isolates the
protein-only prediction response while preserving the assembly that forms the
active sites. It cannot explain substrate-specific activity by itself.

Ten independent inference seeds per sequence is a proposed initial pilot,
not a literature-derived sufficient sample size. Analyze convergence and
within-sequence variability, then extend all nine sequences to 20 seeds if
needed. One or two models per sequence would be a poor basis for separating
subtle mutation effects from stochastic variation. McBride and Tlusty (2024),
Supplementary Section 1.1, report that five structures provided much of the
gain over a single prediction and recommend 20 structures. Their benchmark
does not guarantee sufficient sampling for a flexible hetero-octamer.
No apo inference campaign has been launched by this feasibility assessment.

### Primary and sensitivity definitions

For each substrate, a residue is included in the primary common mask only if
its mean original confidence over all 50 predictions exceeds 70 for every
one of the nine sequences. This ensemble-mean rule is a documented adaptation
of the paper's individual-prediction threshold. It retains 1023/1312 residues
for PA6 and 1025/1312 for PA6,6, including all four copies of Thr1, Phe113,
and Asn186. It excludes all four copies of mutation centers 59 and 101;
mutation 55 lacks alpha C, and mutation 105 is represented in alpha A only
for PA6 and A/B/D for PA6,6. Mutation centers 53 and 111 remain in all copies.
Coverage is therefore part of every regional result, and no missing-center
estimate is replaced by zero.

The stricter common mask requires confidence >70 in every original prediction
of every sequence and retains 792/1312 PA6 and 804/1312 PA6,6 residues.
Both this mask and a fully unmasked calculation are sensitivity analyses.
Additional sensitivities use within-physical-subunit neighborhoods,
relaxed coordinates with original confidence masks, and Tier A/B subsets.
The last comparison is skipped with an explicit reason if either arm has
fewer than five models; otherwise its actual unequal arm sizes are reported.
It is a conditional sensitivity, not a noise-matched primary result.

Primary strain uses all 50 WT predictions and all 50 mutant predictions,
WT as reference, fixed input index order, and a 13 A neighbor radius.
For each arm, neighbors must be present in every model, their vectors are
locally rotated to the first model, then averaged. Shared neighbors between
arms are explicitly aligned by sorted global residue index before the final
local rotation and reference-normalized vector-residual calculation.
The memory-efficient adapter uses upstream `rotate_points` and agrees
numerically with upstream `AverageProtein`/`Deformation` on multi-model tests;
the scientific formula and per-arm intersection order are preserved.
Reference direction and averaging order are retained in the reproducibility
record because finite deformation need not be symmetric or order invariant.

Summaries require at least three common neighbors at a residue. Predeclared
regions are all protein residues, four mutation centers, a union of protein
residues within 15 A of any mutation copy, each copy's 15 A neighborhood,
and the twelve active-site core residues (alpha113/186 and beta1 at four sites).
Region distances are averaged within-model WT distances, not distances
between globally averaged coordinates. Neighborhood overlap is retained;
per-copy summaries are descriptive and not independent replicates.

### Noise, sampling, and interpretation

For each mutant/substrate, 20 random partitions create disjoint WT 25+25 and
mutant 25+25 splits, plus a matched-size WT25-versus-mutant25 comparison.
These directly compare between-sequence deformation with prediction noise
at the same ensemble size. Repeated partitions overlap across repetitions
and are not independent biological observations or formal significance tests.

One hundred bootstrap draws independently resample each complete 50-model
arm with replacement. Three subset draws without replacement at each of
5, 10, 20, and 25 models assess convergence toward the full 50-model result.
Seeds are fixed at `20261015 + variant_index`, with the explicit sequence
order in the source. Bootstrap percentiles quantify prediction-ensemble
sampling uncertainty only and have limited Monte Carlo precision at 100 draws.
Confidence masks and biological regions stay fixed during resampling, while
neighbor intersections and evaluable coverage are recalculated. Convergence
must be examined together with coverage because increasing ensemble size can
remove neighbors that were not common to every prediction.

Do not interpret strain magnitude or a positive confidence interval as proof
of a genuine mutation response: effective strain is nonnegative even under
within-sequence noise. Evaluate the matched-size controls, confidence coverage,
subunit behavior, and substrate dependence. This analysis does not validate
Boltz mutant coordinates against experimental structures or provide a
calibrated catalytic-activity prediction.

### Interpreting the four mutation copies

Effective strain is dimensionless deformation, not a strain energy. The
`mutation_sites` summary is the mean of the strain values at the four mapped
alpha mutation positions, restricted to residues with confidence inclusion
and at least three shared neighbors. The evaluated/expected count identifies
partial coverage; an unavailable site is never counted as zero. Per-residue
tables retain the individual copies, and 15 A neighborhood summaries are
reported for A, B, C, and D separately. The union-based `mutation_15A` summary
counts each included residue once; it is not the equal-weight average of four
overlapping neighborhood means. Preserve these distinctions when preparing
figures. Site asymmetry, including substrate-adjacent versus other sites,
can be hidden by a symmetry average. All resampling is over whole models,
keeping four mutation copies together; copies are not independent replicates.

## 2026-10-16 interpretation, presentation statistic, and redesign implications

### What the analysis establishes about mutant-model quality

The predicted mutant ensembles differ from WT more than the matched-size
within-WT sampling baseline in the mutation neighborhoods examined. This is
evidence of reproducible sequence-associated differences within this prediction
workflow, not experimental validation of those differences. Shared systematic
errors remain possible, and effective strain cannot classify individual models
as accurate or inaccurate. Small deformation likewise does not establish
correctness, folding stability, or catalytic competence.

Strain has not supplied an explanation for the poor PA6,6 static directionality.
This does not exclude structural prediction errors as a contributor. The
calculation uses protein C-alpha coordinates, not substrate atoms or side-chain
atoms. Substrate register, side-chain packing, hydrogen-bond geometry, and
near-attack geometry can be wrong without large protein-backbone strain.
Reproducible deformation alone does not validate an interface_delta ranking or
explain lysate activity. No formal strain-versus-activity or strain-versus-local-
confidence correlation has been established by the current analysis.

### Relationship with confidence and magnitude

Confidence and deformation are different observables. Low-confidence regions
can inflate deformation through inconsistent predictions, while high confidence
does not preclude a reproducible structural change or systematic error.
McBride et al. (2023; reference above) use confidence filtering and ensemble
averaging to address prediction variability. Our transfer of that approach to
Boltz and this oligomeric enzyme is not a validated activity predictor.

For PA6 F101E, the full-50 mutation-neighborhood mean is 0.0077455 with the
primary common confidence mask, 0.0067244 with the strict mask, and 0.1059994
without masking. Relaxed coordinates with the primary mask give 0.0228686.
These comparisons change included residues and/or their neighbor sets, so they
demonstrate sensitivity rather than measuring a pure confidence effect at fixed
coverage. Residue 101 has ensemble-mean original confidence of approximately
43.5-45.3 in WT and 48.9-50.4 in F101E across A-D; its four copies are excluded
from the primary mask, while retained surrounding residues contribute.

Effective strain is a mean reference-normalized local vector displacement after
local rotational alignment. A value of 0.015 is a dimensionless normalized
deformation of about 1.5%, not a displacement of 0.015 A, an energy, a uniform
bond extension, or a calibrated biological effect. There is no established
Nyl12 threshold for biologically large or small strain. In the slide drafts,
values are multiplied by 1,000, so a displayed value of 15 means 0.015.

### Exact meaning of the presentation dots and table

Reproducible exporter: `scripts/plot_nyl12_effective_strain_presentation.py`.
Artifacts: `output_nyl12_effective_strain_20261015/presentation/`, including
PNG/PDF/SVG dot plot and table, numeric CSV, and three-sentence speaker notes.
Sources are each substrate's `sampling_diagnostics.csv`; the exporter uses
`region == mutation_15A`, `matched25_median`, and `WT_split_median`.

For each mutant/substrate, the colored dot is calculated as follows:

1. Define a fixed WT-based union of protein C-alpha positions within 15 A of
   any of the four alpha mutation positions; count overlapping residues once.
2. For each of 20 comparisons, select 25 WT and 25 mutant whole assemblies
   without replacement from their respective 50-model ensembles. Calculate
   per-residue strain between locally averaged ensemble neighborhoods, using
   the fixed confidence mask and 13 A common-neighbor rule.
3. Average the strain over evaluable residues in the union, requiring at least
   three shared neighbors per residue, then take the median of those 20
   regional means. Multiply by 1,000 for display. The table reports the same
   statistic rounded to one decimal in displayed units.

Gray points are the corresponding medians of 20 disjoint 25-versus-25 WT
partitions. Partitions overlap between repetitions. These are diagnostics of
prediction sampling, not independent biological replicates or formal tests.
The presentation statistic differs slightly from the full-50 regional mean.

The dots are NOT averages of four mutation-center strains. `mutation_sites`
is a separate summary with partial or missing coverage at several positions.
Neither are the dots equal-weight averages of the four overlapping per-copy
15 A neighborhood means. Therefore, an SD or SEM of four center values would
not be an appropriate error bar for this plot.

To show subunit asymmetry, plot the four labeled per-copy neighborhoods (or
individual mutation centers with explicit coverage) as descriptive values in
a separate panel, noting overlap and dependence. To show prediction-sampling
spread around the existing dots, use the corresponding 20 matched-25 regional
means and label a percentile range explicitly as split-to-split variability,
not a confidence interval or between-chain SD. Existing full-50 bootstrap
percentiles describe another statistic and must not be attached to the
matched-25 medians. An uncertainty interval for the matched-25 median would
require a resampling procedure targeting that estimator. No error bars have
been added to the current prototype pending a choice of which variation to
communicate; absence of bars does not imply zero variability.

### Implications for LigandMPNN designs with multiple substitutions

LigandMPNN conditions sequence design on a supplied backbone and atomic
context; many compatible substitutions can therefore preserve the intended
backbone, whereas a single incompatible substitution may cause a larger
change. Mutation count does not imply monotonically increasing or decreasing
effective strain. Compensating substitutions could reduce deformation, but
that is a design hypothesis, not a guaranteed consequence of LigandMPNN.
Low geometric strain is not synonymous with low energetic strain or improved
stability. Reference: Dauparas et al., Atomic context-conditioned protein
sequence design using LigandMPNN, Nature Methods (2025), DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02626-1; implementation:
https://github.com/dauparas/LigandMPNN.

For a future pilot of 10-20 designed sequences, predict repeated structures
per sequence and compare them with both the actual design backbone and
matched WT ensembles. Retain catalytic identities, assess their orientations
and supporting contacts, and enforce equivalent sequence choices in the four
copies of each physical subunit. Evaluate catalytic geometry, ligand register,
assembly interfaces, local confidence, and reproducibility alongside strain.
Use fixed catalytic/interface evaluation regions shared by all designs;
expanding a mutation-neighborhood union with mutation count can change or
dilute the summary. Retaining catalytic identities alone does not preserve
their geometry. These are recommendations; no redesign campaign is launched.

Suggested cautious slide title: "Predicted local deformation is greatest for
T53Q and P105K." This is more directly supported than interpreting above-noise
deformation as validated mutation-induced structural change.
