## Experiment and interpretation

- The tested substrate-hydrolysis mutant was **K189A in His-tagged NylC-p2
  G122Y130A36Q263 (H-GYAQ)**, not wild-type NylC. Enzyme activity was measured
  against Ahx cyclic oligomer at 30 °C (Table 1 footnote b; PDF p. 3 / journal
  p. 3402).
- K189A cleavage was 6.5–9.7% initially and reached 33% after successive
  in-vitro incubation. Table 1 reports 0.032 U mg⁻¹ (~0.6% of parental H-GYAQ)
  after dividing specific activity by cleavage ratio to estimate activity of
  completely cleaved enzyme (Table 1 footnote d; PDF p. 3 / journal p. 3402).
  Thus incomplete maturation is
  an explicit confound for raw activity, partly adjusted by the authors; the
  cleavage-normalized estimate supports a severe hydrolysis-associated defect,
  but does not prove altered chemistry alone explains it.
- TLC showed trace Ahx-dimer for K189A after 24 h, while parental H-GYAQ produced
  dimer and monomer within 1 h (Fig. S12, slots 7, 9 vs 10). This supports
  impaired substrate conversion in addition to slow autocleavage (Fig. S12,
  supplemental figure).
- The precursor structure resolves Wat67 2.85 Å from Lys189 (Fig. 6C). A
  Lys189–water–Thr267 hydrogen-bond network and a general acid–base role are
  proposed from structure, mutagenesis, and MD (Wat67: Fig. 6C, PDF p. 9 /
  journal p. 3408; discussion and Fig. 7B: PDF pp. 11–12 / journal pp. 3410–11;
  Fig. 10: PDF p. 17 / journal p. 3416); proton transfer
  and a specific chemical step were not directly observed. Fig. 10B is a
  substrate-interaction model, not a substrate-bound crystal structure.
- The earlier NylC structural/mechanism study by Negoro et al. (*JBC*, 2012,
  DOI [10.1074/jbc.M111.321992](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M111.321992)) proposed
  Thr267-centered catalysis but did not report Lys189 or K189A. The 2023 paper
  notes Lys189 is not conserved among other Ntn hydrolases (Fig. 10A/S3).

## Nyl12 scope

Project mapping places NylC Lys189 at Nyl12 Lys156. This makes K156 protection a
precaution against disrupting a residue analogous to one important for NylC
function/hydrolysis. It does not establish a Nyl12-specific substrate contact,
proton-transfer role, or catalytic necessity: no direct Nyl12 K156-mutant
evidence was identified in this audit. The exact Lys189 chemistry in NylC also
remains proposed.
